\begin{abstract}
Vision--language--action (VLA) safety is judged at two endpoints, the instruction and the end state, and neither constrains \emph{how} a task is carried out. We define \textbf{execution-phase safety}, harm done while a nominally safe task is completed, along four dimensions of a motion: where it goes, how its payload is oriented, how fast and hard it meets a person, and whether it reacts when they move. Each sub-type is scored with a trajectory predicate, mostly person-referenced, and read, where available, against a person-blind control (a scripted straight-line carry) and a witness showing a compliant completion exists. Across four DROID-trained policies ($\pi_{0.5}$, $\pi_0$, $\pi_0$-FAST, GR00T N1.6-DROID) on a simulated Franka at six work surfaces, \textbf{none is detectably safer than the person-blind control on any sub-type both ran}; all four bow into a keep-out beside the transport that the control rarely enters ($\pi_{0.5}$ 90/159 against 18/160), and no other difference survives Holm correction over 12 matched comparisons; a pre-registered test adds the body sweep, $\pi_{0.5}$'s and $\pi_0$-FAST's arms coming within 0.10 m of a bystander beside the bowl on 26/60 and 34/64 episodes, the control's on 0/64. A hazard's heading follows its spawn pose and the carry, not the person, and $\pi_{0.5}$ carries into a coworker's forearm crossing the transport on 57/88 and waits on 7/88 ($\pi_0$-FAST 58/62, 0/62). Safety language can move the motion the wrong way: told not to spill the coffee, $\pi_{0.5}$ and $\pi_0$-FAST tilt the mug past 45$^{\circ}$ on 30/32 and 14/27 carries (neutral 3/32, 0/32), $\pi_0$ on 10/12 (1/13; mostly one cell), GR00T N1.6-DROID not detectably (2/9, 6/12), while ``keep the mug upright'' alone does not detectably raise $\pi_{0.5}$'s or $\pi_0$-FAST's (5/32, 1/31). A replication pre-registered on fresh seeds ($\pi_{0.5}$, $\pi_0$-FAST) holds every prediction whose reference was valid. A humanoid case study (GR00T N1.6, Unitree G1; no person-blind control) shows a similar pattern. Scenes, metrics, scorer tests and per-episode logs are released.
\end{abstract}
