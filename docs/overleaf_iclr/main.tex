\begin{abstract}
Vision--language--action (VLA) safety is judged at two endpoints: whether the instruction should be followed and whether the end state is acceptable. Neither constrains \emph{how} the task is carried out. We define \textbf{execution-phase safety} --- harm done while a nominally safe task is completed: along the path, in the pose, at the speed, with the body, with the load, or by not reacting to a moving person --- as a third axis, and give it a six-type taxonomy (T1--T6), each type a measurable tuple of harm channel, phase, quantity, violation predicate, metric and fixability class. We instantiate it as a diagnostic benchmark on the cell no existing suite covers --- a locomoting humanoid (GR00T N1.6 on a Unitree G1 in Isaac Sim) carrying a hazard past a passive bystander --- and port two channels to $\pi_{0.5}$ on a Franka arm. Every carry that completes the task passes through the hazard's keep-out zone (GR00T 109/109 completing carries pooled over hazards, positions, seeds and appearance; $\pi_{0.5}$ 22/22, and 16/16 with the hazard rendered visible); explicit safety commands do not reduce this on either policy (paired \emph{N} = 16--24, McNemar \emph{p} = 1.0); an oracle-fed external shield does (8/8 $\rightarrow$ 0/8 on the stove; on the other hazards only with a larger margin). The robot's own arm reaches the bystander's body surface ($\pi_{0.5}$ 8/32 episodes; GR00T 8/8 at its worst position), the carried object passes a person at full speed inside the ISO/TS 15066 stop distance (6/6), and a crossing person is walked into --- the carried box stops only on contact with them, on 11/11 completing carries (0/3 off-path). We show that the scoring geometry, not the policy, sets several of these rates, and argue that what a policy owns is not the safety function but the demand it places on it. Scenes, metrics and the per-episode logs behind every reported number are released.
\end{abstract}
