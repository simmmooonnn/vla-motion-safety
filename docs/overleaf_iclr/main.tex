\begin{abstract}
Vision--language--action (VLA) safety is judged at two endpoints --- should the instruction be followed, and is the end state acceptable --- and neither constrains \emph{how} a task is carried out. We define \textbf{execution-phase safety}, harm done while a nominally safe task is completed, along four dimensions of a motion: where it goes (trajectory), how its payload is oriented, how fast and how hard it meets a person (speed and force), and whether it reacts when the person moves (dynamics). A diagnostic benchmark scores each sub-type with a human-referenced predicate and attributes it with two references: a person-blind straight-line carrier, which sets the rate a scene forces, and a witness showing that a compliant completion exists. On a Franka arm driven by four DROID-trained policies ($\pi_{0.5}$, $\pi_0$, $\pi_0$-FAST, GR00T N1.6-DROID) at six work surfaces, \textbf{no policy is detectably safer than the person-blind carrier on any sub-type both ran, and several are worse}: every policy enters a keep-out beside the transport more often than a straight carry ($\pi_{0.5}$ 90/159 against 18/160), $\pi_{0.5}$ sweeps its links into a person beside the bowl more often (20/64 against 5/32, at a 0.10 m link-origin margin), and GR00T N1.6-DROID tilts a mug more often (7/18 against 3/31). A hazard's orientation stays frozen at its spawn pose wherever the person stands, payloads pass people at full speed, and a coworker's forearm crossing the transport is carried into on 57/88 episodes and waited for on 7/88. A humanoid case study (GR00T N1.6, Unitree G1) carries a box into the keep-out of a stove 0.28 m off its path on 11/30 completing carries. Naming or rendering the hazard does not detectably lower the violation rate, and in one session telling $\pi_{0.5}$ to keep hot coffee upright tilts the mug past 45$^{\circ}$ on 13/13 carries. Scenes, metrics, task cards and per-episode logs are released.
\end{abstract}
