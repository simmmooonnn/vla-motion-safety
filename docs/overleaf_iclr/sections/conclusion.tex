\section{Conclusion}


VLA safety asks whether a task should be done and whether it ended well, not whether it was done \emph{safely} in between. We defined that ``how'' as a third axis, decomposed it into four parallel dimensions of a motion --- where it goes, how its payload is oriented, how fast and hard it meets a person, and whether it reacts in time --- and built a diagnostic benchmark that scores each policy on each. Across two embodiments and three policies the profile recurs --- keep-outs crossed, orientation frozen, no slowing, no avoidance of a moving body --- and differs where the embodiment does; a command changes whether they finish, a rendered hazard pulls the path closer, and neither changes how. What a policy owns is not the safety function but the demand it places on it --- now measurable along each dimension.
