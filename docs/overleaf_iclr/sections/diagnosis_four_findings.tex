\section{Diagnosis: Four Findings}


Table~\ref{tab:IIIf} says the policies are no safer than a person-blind line; the ablations ask whether a prompt or a percept can move them, and it is there that execution-phase safety departs from collision avoidance.

\textbf{(i) A safety command changes completion, not the violation.} Among completing carries an explicit safety command leaves the violation unchanged (T1, a hazard on the path: 8/8 and 7/7; T3: 8/12 and 11/14); per attempt the rates move only with completion (T1 8/24 $\rightarrow$ 7/24, T3 8/24 $\rightarrow$ 11/24, T6 30 $\rightarrow$ 25 \%; Fig. \ref{fig:fixability}). With the stove 0.28 m off the path, neither naming nor rendering lowers the violation rate (Table~\ref{tab:XI}); each shifts completing carries by about 3 cm --- naming away from a hidden stove (Mann--Whitney \emph{p} = 0.017), rendering toward it (\emph{p} = 0.013, 0.005) --- among completers whose share the arms change (29--68 \%). In one session keep-the-hot-coffee-upright tilts the mug past 45$^{\circ}$ on 13/13 carries against 2/15 (two cells per arm; E.8).

\textbf{(ii) A visible hazard does not repel the path; the arm's path drifts whether or not one is there.} The humanoid's 2--3 cm shift toward a rendered stove comes from one two-seed experiment run on the substitute-driver day (E.2) and leaves the violation rate statistically unchanged (17/51 against 15/83, \emph{p} = 0.06). On $\pi_{0.5}$ a keep-out 0.28 m off the transport, which the blind carrier clears 0/64, is entered on 12/64 --- on the far side only, rendered or not (near side 0/32, far side \emph{unrendered} 9/32): the bend is a pull toward the demonstrations' radius (E.8), not toward what is seen.

\textbf{(iii) Neither orientation nor speed is conditioned on the person.} The carry yaw is the same at every bystander azimuth for GR00T, on either side of the table for $\pi_{0.5}$ (T3) and at any rendering of the person on either embodiment (E.7, E.8); the carry speed shows no detectable change with the person present (T5a). The yaw follows the object's initial pose. On the humanoid the base command shows no detectable difference with a bystander present (12 episode pairs, \emph{p} = 0.28; E.1), a test with little power against a local detour.

\textbf{(iv) A moving person is walked into, and one who stops is struck, not avoided.} No deceleration precedes contact at any crossing speed (T6), and a person who stops at the first touch is struck on every carried encounter (\S5.4; a kinematic body, so the force is an upper bound). That the competences are \emph{absent from the imitation training distribution} (\S2) is consistent with every ablation here; it is not demonstrated by them.
