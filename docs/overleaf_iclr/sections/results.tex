\subsection{Trajectory: T1 payload path, T2 body sweep}


\textbf{T1: completing carries pass through the keep-out.} On the humanoid the corridor's hazards stand on the carry path, so 121/125 completing carries cross them --- exposure; the scored G1 T1 is the stove placed 0.28 m off the path, where 11/30 completing carries enter its 0.30 m keep-out (Table~\ref{tab:XI}; 12/16 on two new seeds, E.2). A shield given the hazard's coordinates clears it (8/8 $\rightarrow$ 0/8, completion kept; Fig. \ref{fig:shield}), a feasibility witness (E.2). On the tabletop the scored T1 is a marker or a bystander's resting hand 0.20 or 0.28 m \emph{beside} the transport: the control's line grazes the first by millimetres (18/80) and clears the second (0/80), and $\pi_{0.5}$ enters them on 87/93 and 12/94 (99/187). Whichever side the keep-out is on, the policies' carries bend to the far side (mean mid-transport offset 4--6 cm, GR00T N1.6-DROID 13, the control $-$0.1; E.8), $\pi_0$'s to the near side at the packing station. The excess rests on a Cartesian control: a pre-registered blind carry interpolating in joint space between the same poses bends the same way, enters every far 0.20 m keep-out and no 0.28 m one, and $\pi_{0.5}$ does not detectably exceed it (90/159 against 68/132, \emph{p} = 0.169; at 0.28 m 12/80 against 0/64, \emph{p} = 0.093); only GR00T N1.6-DROID does, in 90 s episodes (25/25 against 26/51, Holm 0.025; C). A marker \emph{on} the line is crossed by any transport: exposure (56/56).

\textbf{T2: the robot's own body reaches the bystander.} With the bystander beside the humanoid's workspace (four positions, \emph{N} = 8 each), a robot link comes within 0.10 m of the body surface on 27/32 episodes and touches it on 19/32, where a margin to the person's \emph{axis} registers only 8/32; the rate is set by the scoring geometry as much as by the policy (Fig. \ref{fig:t4thr}, E.3). The scored tabletop T2 is therefore the serving geometry (three goals, Table~\ref{tab:IVe}); with the bowl away from the body $\pi_{0.5}$'s rate floors at 3/605, and those cells score no T2. \textbf{Against the control, three of the four policies' arms sweep the person.} In a pre-registered test on the serving mug cell (eight fresh seeds; the control's earlier serving cells had run $\pi_{0.5}$, C), the control never brings a link within 0.10 m of the body (0/64); $\pi_{0.5}$ does on 26/60 episodes and $\pi_0$-FAST on 34/64 (cell permutation \emph{p} < 0.001 each, Holm 0.0003). On the same cell and seeds GR00T N1.6-DROID's arm does on 51/54 in its 90 s episodes (43/58 in 35 s ones), carrying the mug or not (Holm 0.0003; uncarried 31/32), and $\pi_0$'s on none (0/57, as predicted), a competence limit: its lifted mug reaches the bowl on 3/57. $\pi_{0.5}$'s and $\pi_0$-FAST's entries come at the bowl, while placing; among delivered episodes, where the control places the mug too, the excess holds ($\pi_{0.5}$ 26/56, $\pi_0$-FAST 32/56, GR00T N1.6-DROID 14/14, against 0/24; post hoc, \emph{p} $\leq$ 0.0003), on a thin margin (the control's placements pass 0.13--0.19 m from the body; C).


\subsection{Orientation: T3 hazard presentation, T4 load tilt}


\textbf{T3: the payload's orientation ignores the person.} Across eight bystander azimuths (\emph{N} = 8 each) GR00T holds a fixed carry yaw whatever the person's position; treating the box's long axis as the hazardous axis, it points into their half-space on 14/27 completing carries --- chance --- and on 20/20 at the two azimuths the frozen axis faces (11/11 with a command to keep the knife away). $\pi_{0.5}$ carrying scissors does the same: its carry yaw (mean -40$^{\circ}$ and -41$^{\circ}$ with the person left and right) does not follow the person, so the blade tip points into their half-space on 0/10 carries with the person right, 10/10 left (E.8). Pooled over the 14 hazardous-axis tasks with a still bystander, the rate is 275/468 = 59 \% [50, 67]*, close to the person-blind control's on the same placements (50/74 against 79/128; Table~\ref{tab:IIIf}): the scored T3, a property of the carry, not of the person. Turning the scissors' initial pose by 180$^{\circ}$ moves the violation to the other side (10/13 right, 2/15 left; near chance at the other placements): the spawn sets the side where the frozen yaw aligns with the bearing. A scripted carry that turns the scissors blade-away delivers them that way on 31/32 carries: with the 5 of 10 carries on the right delivered blade-away as spawned, the tabletop T3 witness (E.8).

\textbf{T4: an upright carry, until spilling is mentioned.} $\pi_{0.5}$ mostly carries a mug upright: pooled over the 18 spillable-vessel tasks with a still bystander, its axis leaves upright by more than 45$^{\circ}$ mid-transport on 78/996 = 8 \% [6, 10] (GR00T N1.6-DROID 36/97; 18/57 within 35 s). Told to keep hot coffee upright so it does not spill, $\pi_{0.5}$ tilts the mug more, not less: over three prompt experiments a spill clause puts it past 45$^{\circ}$ on 141/170 carries against 6/79 neutral (a length-matched irrelevant sentence 2/32), mostly late in the carry (15/170 against 1/79 before half-way), and $\pi_0$-FAST's on 33/64 against 4/48 (cell permutation stratified by experiment, \emph{p} = 2 $\times$ $10^{-7}$ and 0.001; $\pi_0$-FAST's from one experiment; E.8). Of seven phrasings, ``do not spill the coffee'' gives 30/32 past 45$^{\circ}$ ($\pi_0$-FAST 14/27) against 3/32 (0/32) neutral and 5/32 (1/31) for ``keep the mug upright'' alone (cell permutation against the latter \emph{p} = 0.029 per policy, the floor; Table~\ref{tab:XIII}). GR00T's rigid box stays near-level in transit (0/17). The person-blind control, pinching the same mug (\S5.5), exceeds 45$^{\circ}$ on 3/31 carries: the tabletop T4 witness and Table~\ref{tab:IIIf}'s T4 control (E.5, E.8).


\subsection{Speed and force: T5}


\textbf{T5a: no speed-and-separation behavior.} A matched present-versus-absent design removes the trajectory confound: 0.340 $\pm$ 0.029 m/s with the person present, 0.367 $\pm$ 0.006 m/s without (Welch \emph{p} $\approx$ 0.06, six carries) --- no detectable modulation; the point estimate is 7 \% slower with the person. Against the ISO/TS 15066 envelope \citep{ref11} ($T_r + T_s = 0.4$ s, $C = 0.2$ m, $Z = 0.1$ m, walking human) every completing carry passes the person at $\approx$ 0.34 m/s inside the $d_0 = 0.94$ m at which a stop is required (6/6 matched, 16/16 on the E.2 person cell; Fig. \ref{fig:ssm}). The count is partly scene-set (no 1.9 m traversal stays outside $d_0$); the policy's own finding is the absence of slowing. A strict governor with the 0.60 m shield completes 3/6 carries inside the envelope for a still person ($v_h = 0$; E.6), against which the policy's count is unchanged: every carry passes within $C + Z$ = 0.30 m of the person. A table-side arm works inside $d_0$, so on the tabletop T5a is exposure (255/272 transports above the envelope), a reading that rests on the walking-human term (at $v_h = 0$ every arm is near zero: $\pi_{0.5}$ 2/272, the control 2/208; E.8); power-and-force limiting, which applies there, is exposure too (\S4.2). Its near-band speed shows no detectable change with the person there (0.109 vs 0.113 m/s, \emph{p} = 0.79; E.8). $\pi_{0.5}$'s transport is not slowed within 0.60 m of the person (mean speed there at least 0.8 of its speed beyond) on 39/57 carries, against 28/52 in a person-hidden twin (Fisher \emph{p} = 0.17): T5s, a speed member beside the score (no witness).

\textbf{T5b: the force that reaches the person.} The crossing person's contact sensor registers a contact on every carried encounter (21/21, median 148 N), and a tabletop hand is touched on 65/83 carried episodes; both proxies are kinematic, so these are constraint forces with no compliance behind them and T5b is exposure on both embodiments (E.7, E.8).

\textbf{T5c: a hazardous end that carries speed (tool tasks; beside the score).} Holding a ladle, a spatula or tongs, $\pi_{0.5}$ drives the hazardous end at a median peak of 0.54 m/s (max 1.30) within 0.40 m of the adult, and above 0.25 m/s inside 0.5 m of them on 5/41 carried episodes (threshold set post hoc; Table~\ref{tab:IVc}, E.8).


\subsection{Dynamics: T6 moving person}


\textbf{T6: a moving person is walked into.} A kinematic capsule with a collider crosses the corridor at a creeping 0.06 m/s (walking speed in E.7). On every completing on-path carry in three seeds (11/11) and on 10/13 in three more runs, the payload reaches the person without slowing (0.25--0.37 m/s one step before; Fig. \ref{fig:t6contact}). At 0.3--1.2 m/s the person knocks it from the grasp (16/23 carried encounters reach contact), never after a deceleration; one who stops at first contact is struck on every carried encounter (9/9, median 244 N). A protective stop at 0.50 m fires on 22/24 with no carried contact (0/11): the scene's witness and the layer's demand (E.7). On the tabletop the scored T6 is a coworker's forearm crossing the transport line ahead of the payload: $\pi_{0.5}$ carries into it on 57/88 and waits on 7/88 (pouring included; $\pi_0$-FAST 58/62, 0/62), into the hand neither rendered nor colliding on 22/27 (visible at that table 20/24, \emph{p} = 1.0), and a whole-arm stop removes the contact (0/16) and still delivers (16/16): the null and the witness (E.8). The payload is lowered onto a hand reaching into the bowl on 81/83 carried episodes, but delivery itself demands it (the person-blind control 16/16): exposure. \textbf{T6b}: a person walking past the table at 0.55 m/s is passed at $\geq$ 80 \% of the transport speed on 97/125 carried episodes, against 70 \% without a passer-by, read at the same moment (binomial \emph{p} = 0.06): no anticipatory slowing, as on the G1, where 17/18 scored encounters show no prior deceleration. Against a person approaching at 1.2 m/s, as the ISO envelope assumes, and stopping 0.5 m short, 17/25 carries keep 80 \% of their speed.


\subsection{Across policies and embodiments}


Across five policies and two embodiments the profile recurs where measured (Table~\ref{tab:III}; Fig. \ref{fig:heatmap}): keep-outs entered, a hazard's orientation frozen, no detectable slowing near people, no avoidance of a moving body. It differs where the embodiment does --- an arm tilts a cup the rigid box could not show (GR00T N1.6-DROID, 37 \%) --- and, on the body sweep, where the \emph{policy} does: GR00T N1.6-DROID's arm sweeps the body on 78 \% of person-side serving episodes and $\pi_0$'s on 2 \%, seldom reaching the bowl (the humanoid, in its own scene, on 84 \%) --- and where the task does: a body sweep needs a destination beside the person, which is why T2 is scored there ($\pi_{0.5}$ 14 \%, its person-side cells 31 \%). $\pi_0$ carries on 321/1207 episodes and GR00T N1.6-DROID on 242/803; where they carry, both repeat the pattern (E.8).

\textbf{The person-blind control.} Its geometric variant reads simulator state, ignores the person and carries an attached payload along a Cartesian line with an IK-driven arm; it supplies the T1--T3 comparisons (T2's pre-registered, \S5.1) and the T1 and T2 witnesses; its tilt and contact cells carry no attribution. For T4 a matched variant drops the attachment and pinches the mug (31/112 carried; \S5.2). \textbf{Where a safer verdict was attainable, no policy is detectably safer than this control} (Fig. \ref{fig:forest}, Table~\ref{tab:IIIf}; $\pi_{0.5}$'s T3 is at most 21 points safer, one-sided 95 \%); the openpi policies' T4 and the small T3 rows could not have shown one: untested, not null. Every policy enters more keep-outs than the Cartesian line, only GR00T N1.6-DROID (over 90 s) more than a joint-space one, three sweep the body more (\S5.1), and no T3 or T4 difference survives Holm correction.
