\subsection{Trajectory: T1 payload path, T2 body sweep}


\textbf{T1: completing carries pass through the keep-out.} On the humanoid the corridor's hazards stand on the carry path, so 121/125 completing carries cross them --- exposure, as the tabletop's on-path marker is; the scored G1 T1 is the stove placed 0.28 m off the path, where the rate has headroom: 11/30 completing carries enter its 0.30 m keep-out (Table~\ref{tab:XI}). A repulsion shield given the hazard's coordinates clears the on-path hazard (8/8 $\rightarrow$ 0/8, \emph{p} = 1.6 $\times$ $10^{-4}$, completion kept; Fig. \ref{fig:shield}) --- the witness that a clearing path exists (E.2). On the tabletop the scored T1 is a marker or a bystander's resting hand 0.20 or 0.28 m \emph{beside} the transport: a direct carry grazes the first (the blind carrier enters it on 18/80) and clears the second (0/80), and $\pi_{0.5}$ enters them on 87/93 and 12/94 (99/187 in all) --- the policy's bow toward the far side, present without any keep-out, sets the rate (E.8). A marker \emph{on} the line is crossed by any transport, so that cell (56/56) is exposure (E.8).

\textbf{T2: the robot's own body reaches the bystander.} With the bystander beside the humanoid's workspace (four positions, \emph{N} = 8 each), a robot link comes within 0.10 m of the body surface on 27/32 episodes and touches it on 19/32, where a margin to the person's \emph{axis} registers only 8/32; the rate is set by the scoring geometry as much as by the policy (threshold curve: Fig. \ref{fig:t4thr}, E.3). The scored tabletop T2 is therefore the serving geometry, three goals (Table~\ref{tab:IVe}): on pick-and-place, $\pi_{0.5}$ enters the band on 47/284 episodes over 36 cells at three work surfaces, $\pi_0$-FAST on 16/96, the blind carrier on 5/32. With the bowl away from the body no link need approach it and the rate floors at 3/605, the mirror image of the on-path marker's ceiling, so those cells are exposure. \textbf{Against the blind carrier on the placements it ran,} $\pi_{0.5}$ sweeps the body more often (20/64 against 5/32; cell-level permutation p = 0.013) at a 0.10 m link-origin margin, not at the 0.14--0.18 m a link's surface implies (E); $\pi_0$ (1/48) and $\pi_0$-FAST (9/32) cannot be told from it, and GR00T N1.6-DROID sweeps it on 25/32 (p = 0.056, the smallest four cells per arm allow) (Table~\ref{tab:IIIf}).


\subsection{Orientation: T3 hazard presentation, T4 load tilt}


\textbf{T3: the payload's orientation ignores the person.} Across eight bystander azimuths (\emph{N} = 8 each) GR00T holds a fixed carry yaw (circular mean +3$^{\circ}$, s.d. 11$^{\circ}$) whatever the person's position; treating the box's long axis as the hazardous axis, it points into their half-space on 14/27 completing carries --- chance --- and on 20/20 at the two azimuths the frozen axis faces (11/11 with a command to keep the knife away). $\pi_{0.5}$ carrying scissors does the same: its carry yaw (circular mean -40$^{\circ}$ and -41$^{\circ}$ with the person left and right) does not follow the person, so the blade tip points into their half-space on 0/10 carries with the person on the right and 10/10 on the left (Fisher \emph{p} < 0.001; a blades-away command ran only on the spared side, E.8). Pooled over every task in which a hazardous-axis payload is carried past a still bystander (14 tasks) the rate is 275/468 = 59 \% [50, 67]*, close to the person-blind control's on the same placements (54/93 against 80/138; Table~\ref{tab:IIIf}): the scored T3, a property of the carry rather than of the person. Turning their initial pose by 180$^{\circ}$ moves the violation to the other side (10/13 right, 2/15 left, at 90$^{\circ}$ 8/10 and 1/7): the object's pose sets it, not the person's, and as spawned 5 carries deliver them blade-away past the person on the right. Across the far edge and at the far-right corner the rotation leaves the rate near chance (E.8): the spawn sets the side where the frozen yaw aligns with the bearing. A scripted carry that turns the scissors blade-away delivers them that way on 31/32 carries (into the half-space on 0/16 with the person right, 0/16 left): the tabletop T3 witness (Appendix E.4, E.8).

\textbf{T4: an upright carry, until upright is asked for.} $\pi_{0.5}$ mostly carries a mug upright: pooled over every task in which it carries a spillable vessel past a still bystander (18 tasks) its axis leaves upright by more than 45$^{\circ}$ mid-transport on 78/996 = 8 \% [6, 10], and by more than a full cup's 14--27$^{\circ}$ spill angle on 212/996 (21 \%); GR00T N1.6-DROID on 36/97. Told to keep hot coffee upright, $\pi_{0.5}$ tilts the mug more, not less: 13/13 against 2/15 with both instructions run back to back (Fisher \emph{p} < 0.001), and on new seeds the sentence alone gives 27/32 against 1/32 (a length-matched irrelevant sentence 2/32; E.8). GR00T's rigid box stays near-level in transit (0/17). A person-blind straight-line control disables the attachment (\texttt{SC\_MAGIC=0}) and physically pinches the same mug: across 112 attempts it carries 31 and exceeds 45$^{\circ}$ on 3/31 --- the tabletop T4 witness and Table~\ref{tab:IIIf}'s T4 control (Appendix E.5, E.8).


\subsection{Speed and force: T5}


\textbf{T5a: no speed-and-separation behavior.} A matched present-versus-absent design removes the trajectory confound: 0.340 $\pm$ 0.029 m/s with the person present, 0.367 $\pm$ 0.006 m/s without (Welch \emph{p} $\approx$ 0.06, six carries) --- no detectable modulation; the point estimate is 7 \% slower with the person. Against the ISO/TS 15066 envelope \citep{ref11} ($T_r + T_s = 0.4$ s, $C = 0.2$ m, $Z = 0.1$ m, walking human) every completing carry passes the person at $\approx$ 0.34 m/s inside the $d_0 = 0.94$ m at which a stop is required (6/6 matched, 16/16 on the E.2 person cell; Fig. \ref{fig:ssm}). The count is partly scene-set (no 1.9 m traversal stays outside $d_0$); the policy's own finding is the absence of slowing. A strict governor with the 0.60 m shield completes 3/6 carries inside the envelope for a still person ($v_h = 0$): the scene admits a compliant carry (E.6), and scored against that same envelope the policy's count is unchanged, since every carry passes within $C + Z$ = 0.30 m of the person. A table-side arm works inside $d_0$, so on the tabletop T5a is exposure (255/272 transports above the envelope): the mode that applies there is power-and-force limiting, whose own cell is exposure too (\S4.2). Its near-band speed shows no detectable change with the person there (0.109 vs 0.113 m/s, \emph{p} = 0.79; E.8).

\textbf{T5b: the force that reaches the person.} A contact sensor on the crossing person of \S5.4 --- a 1.74 m body standing on the floor, four seeds --- registers a contact on every carried encounter: 21/21, peak 55--539 N, median 148 N, duration 0.7 s. The person is a kinematic body, so these are solver constraint forces with no compliance behind them: an upper bound, exposure as on the tabletop, set beside Annex A's torso limits (110--140 N at the payload's height) for scale only. A tabletop placement is slow: the hand is touched on 65/83 carried episodes at peaks up to 260 N, above the 140 N hand limit on 6/83, never above its 280 N transient.

\textbf{T5c: a hazardous end that carries speed (tool tasks; beside the score).} Holding a ladle, a spatula or tongs, $\pi_{0.5}$ drives the hazardous end at a median peak of 0.54 m/s (max 1.30) --- four to ten times its mug-carrying speed --- within 0.40 m of the adult, and above 0.25 m/s inside 0.5 m of them on 5/41 carried episodes. The 0.25 m/s threshold was set post hoc, so the rate is exposure (Table~\ref{tab:IVc}, E.8).


\subsection{Dynamics: T6 moving person}


\textbf{T6: a moving person is walked into.} A kinematic capsule with a collider crosses the corridor at a creeping 0.06 m/s (walking speed in E.7). On every completing on-path carry in three seeds (11/11), 4/5 of a replicate and 6/8 of two further seeds, the payload reaches the person --- 0.26--0.31 m, body radius plus box half-extent --- without slowing (0.25--0.37 m/s one step before; Fig. \ref{fig:t6contact}). With the collider removed no force registers and the box carries on into the body's volume; at 0.3--1.2 m/s the person knocks it from the grasp (16/23 carried encounters reach contact), never after a deceleration; one who stops at first contact is struck on every carried encounter (9/9, median 244 N) and in one kept pressed for 14 s. A protective stop at 0.50 m prevents the contact (0/12 carried with force) and fires on 22/24: the scene's witness and the layer's demand (E.7). On the tabletop the scored T6 is a coworker's forearm crossing the transport line ahead of the payload: $\pi_{0.5}$ carries into it on 57/88 and waits on 7/88 (pouring included; pick-and-place 49/63, 5/63; $\pi_0$-FAST 58/62, 0/62), into the hand neither rendered nor colliding on 22/27 (visible at that table 20/24, \emph{p} = 1.0), and a whole-arm stop removes the contact (0/16) and still delivers (16/16): the null and the witness (E.8). A hand reaching into the bowl is lowered onto on 81/83 carried episodes, but there delivery itself demands the contact (the blind carrier 16/16), so that cell is exposure. \textbf{T6b}: a person walking past the table at 0.55 m/s is passed at $\geq$ 80 \% of the transport speed on 97/125 carried episodes (closest 0.44--0.94 m), where a carry blind to the person, read at the same moment after the lift, would be on 70 \% (binomial \emph{p} = 0.06): no anticipatory slowing, as on the G1, where 17/18 scored encounters show no prior deceleration. Against one who walks \emph{toward} the table at 1.2 m/s and stops 0.5 m short (the approach the ISO envelope assumes), 17/25 carries keep 80 \% of their speed at the closest approach (0.46 m).


\subsection{Across policies and embodiments}


Across five policies and two embodiments the profile recurs where measured (Table~\ref{tab:III}; Fig. \ref{fig:heatmap}): keep-outs crossed, a hazard's orientation frozen, no slowing near people, no avoidance of a moving body. It differs where the embodiment does --- an arm tilts a cup the rigid box could not show (GR00T N1.6-DROID, 37 \%) --- and, on the body sweep, where the \emph{policy} does: GR00T N1.6-DROID's arm sweeps the body on 78 \% of person-side serving episodes against the blind line's 16 \% (\emph{p} = 0.056), the humanoid in its own scene on 84 \% --- and where the task does: a body sweep needs a destination beside the person, which is why T2 is scored there (31 \% with the bowl on the person's side, 3/605 with it away), and it happens at the dining table, almost never at the counter or the desk (0/44, 3/48). The battery's pour, handover and push cells are in E.8. $\pi_0$ carries on 255/877 episodes and GR00T N1.6-DROID on 126/313; where they carry, both repeat the pattern (E.8).

\textbf{Scripted straight-line controls.} The geometric variant reads simulator state, ignores the person and carries with an IK-driven arm and an attached payload (718 carries); it supplies the T1--T3 comparisons, while its tilt and contact cells carry no attribution. For T4 only, a matched variant disables the attachment and physically pinches the mug: 31/112 carries, 3/31 above 45$^{\circ}$. It is also the T1 witness (\S5.1) and T2's control (5/32; E.8). \textbf{On the placements both ran, no policy is detectably safer than this blind line on any sub-type, and several are worse} (Table~\ref{tab:IIIf}): every policy on T1, $\pi_{0.5}$ on T2 (0.10 m margin only) and GR00T N1.6-DROID on T4; on T3 and elsewhere none can be told from it. The sub-type split in each cell, not the dimension score, carries the diagnosis.
