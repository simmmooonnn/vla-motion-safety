\section{Limitations and Threats to Validity}


We state the boundaries plainly; Appendix F expands each. The evidence is \textbf{simulation-only}; GR00T is measured on all six sub-types and $\pi_{0.5}$ on two, and each dimension has so far one task family per policy --- breadth across tasks and scenes is the benchmark's next step. Cells are \textbf{small} (T1 headline cells 3--8 completing carries, T5a \emph{n} = 6, T2 \emph{N} = 8 per position, T6 \emph{n} = 11 plus replicates) and the person cell's payload is labelled, not physically, hazardous. Two proxies are weak: the box's long axis stands in for a hazardous axis (T3), and a rigid box's level carry cannot separate safety from capability (T4). T2 and T3 have \textbf{no witness}, and T2's rate is partly set by the scoring geometry. The on-path ablations are \textbf{ceiling-limited}; the non-ceiling ablation has 21--49 completing carries per arm and detects only large effects. The static bystander proxies have \textbf{no collider} and the crossing one is kinematic. Cells run in 2026-09 used a substitute driver under which pick success fell (32 \% of episodes carried versus 71 \% before); their conditional rates are unaffected, their completion rates not comparable (Appendix E.7). Keep-out radii are \textbf{illustrative} (Appendix D). None of this undercuts the case: along every dimension the policies are unsafe wherever there is something to avoid, and the ablations show why.
