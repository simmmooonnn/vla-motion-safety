\section{Limitations and Threats to Validity}


We state the boundaries plainly; Appendix F expands each. The evidence is \textbf{simulation-only}, mostly one policy on one embodiment, with \textbf{small cells} (T1 headline cells 3--8 completing carries, T3a \emph{n} = 6, T4 \emph{N} = 8 per position, T6 \emph{n} = 11) and a headline person cell of 5/5 with a benign payload: the carried-hazard-past-a-person scene that names our novel cell is instantiated but not yet powered. Three measured rates --- T3a's envelope count, T4 and T6 --- are partly set by the scene or by the scoring geometry and are labelled \textbf{attribution-pending} until a violation-free reference trajectory exists; what is policy-attributable today is the absence of any slowing, detour or stop before contact. The language and perception ablations are \textbf{ceiling-limited}, so ``no detectable change'' is not invariance, and the paired promptability probe is for T1 and T6 a completion-rate comparison (\S6). The shield is \textbf{oracle-fed and margin-tuned} per hazard. The static bystander proxies have \textbf{no collider} and the crossing one records no force; the T4 body model omits the legs and uses link origins. Keep-out radii are \textbf{illustrative, not standards-derived}: a defensible separation under ISO/TS 15066 \citep{ref11} / ISO 13855 \citep{ref20} would be several times larger, which only deepens the violation; the ISO/TS 15066 formulas are carried into ISO 10218-1/-2:2025 \citep{ref35,ref36}, and a domestic humanoid falls under ISO 13482 \citep{ref37}, whose hazard groups T1--T6 refine (Appendix D). LIBERO-Safety \citep{ref24} already places a hand proxy beside a fixed-base arm and perturbs it, so measuring keep-out to a person or reactivity to motion is not new in itself; our claims are the intersection of \S2. None of this undercuts the case: an appearance-robust keep-out defect on two policies, a body-penetration defect, a contact defect under a crossing person and a standards-referenced absence of slowing are enough to show that execution-phase harm is real, systematic and unaddressed by the existing two axes.
