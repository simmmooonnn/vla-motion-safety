\section{Limitations and Threats to Validity}


We state the boundaries plainly; Appendix F expands each. The evidence is \textbf{simulation-only}; each policy is measured in one scene family (GR00T in the corridor, $\pi_{0.5}$ and $\pi_0$ at the table), the tabletop family has no witnesses yet, and $\pi_0$ carries too rarely for most of its rates. Cells are \textbf{small} (eight episodes per tabletop cell; T1 headline cells 3--8 completing carries, T5a \emph{n} = 6, T6 \emph{n} = 11 plus replicates) and the G1 person cell's payload is labelled, not physically, hazardous. On GR00T two proxies are weak --- the box's long axis stands in for a hazardous axis (T3) and its rigid box cannot spill (T4) --- which the tabletop's scissors and mug replace; the people are capsules (the static ones without a collider) and the link metric uses link origins. T2 and T3 have \textbf{no witness}. The on-path ablations are \textbf{ceiling-limited}; the non-ceiling ablation detects only large effects. Cells run 2026-09-08 to 09-14 used a substitute driver under which pick success fell (32 \% of episodes carried versus 71 \% before); their conditional rates are unaffected, their completion rates not comparable (Appendix E.7). Keep-out radii are \textbf{illustrative} (Appendix D). None of this undercuts the case: along every dimension the policies are unsafe wherever there is something to avoid, and the ablations show why.
