Position paper · figure 1 · embodied VLA safety

Execution-phase safety: the third axis

Today's robot-safety work asks whether the command is safe and whether the final state is safe. Neither constrains how the task is carried out. A capable policy can finish a "safe" task while causing harm during execution — and it does.

01 Prior

Instruction safety

Refuse or flag an unsafe command. "Don't pick up the knife to hurt someone."

02 Prior

Outcome safety

Don't reach an unsafe final state. "Don't leave the knife on the baby's chair."

03 This work

Execution-phase safety

Don't cause harm on the way — the path, the pose you present, the force, the sweep, the load, the reaction.

Two of the six types below are already demonstrated empirically — a taxonomy grounded in measurements, not only argument.

The family

Six measurable execution-phase safety types

Each is one tuple: ⟨harm channel · phase · measured quantity · violation predicate · metric-with-CI · fixability⟩.

Fixability is the cross-cutting axis: for T1 the failure is invariant to naming or hiding the hazard (Fisher p=1.0) — so these need architecture / external layers, not better prompts.

Demonstrated · the two anchors

What the two proven channels look like

🛰️ T1 · Path / keep-out complete carry, top-down

Blind drives the box through the keep-out ring; shield routes the same carry around it. 100% violation pooled (CI 81–100%).

🔪 T2 · Presentation orientation handover, top-down

The carried object's hazardous axis (blade) stays aimed at the person — the policy holds a fixed yaw (~0–5°) regardless of which side they stand. "Hand it handle-first" is never learned.

T1 fails by position (plows through a zone); T2 fails by orientation (points the danger at you). Same missing competence, two channels — the position shield fixes only the first.

Proposed · call to action

A benchmark suite, one scene family per type

Each ships a blind-policy defect measurement, the fixability ablations (language / perception / external layer), and a reference safety-layer baseline. Positioned against instruction-refusal & final-state safety; APF (Khatib), CBF (Ames), safe-RL shielding (Alshiekh), ISO/TS 15066.
