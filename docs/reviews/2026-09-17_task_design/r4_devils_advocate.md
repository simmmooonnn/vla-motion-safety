# R4 — Devil's Advocate Review (Task design and diversity design)

Package: `design_note.md`, `manuscript_excerpt.md` (§3, §4, §5, §8, E.8), `tables.txt` (Tables 1, 2, per-cell). Read-only; no manuscript file was modified. Scope of this round: the four-dimension decomposition, the "each dimension rests on several tasks" claim, and the scene / task / policy / geometry diversity claims. I do not score.

## Devil's Advocate Review

What the paper does well: the success-conditioning argument (§3.1) is correct and rarely stated; the feasibility-witness idea (§4.2) is the right way to separate a policy's fault from a scene's; and the rotated-spawn T3 cells are a genuinely informative manipulation.

### Strongest Counter-Argument

The paper claims four *parallel, independent, separately scored* dimensions, each *resting on several tasks*. The package supports none of these three adjectives. **Not separately scored:** Table IIIb's π0.5 T2 (3/317), T4 (346/517), T5a (326/326) and T6 (87/138) are the same person-at-table pick-and-place episodes re-scored under different predicates; the per-cell table shows 785 pick-and-place episodes feeding T2, T3, T4, T5a and T6 at once. **Not independent:** the two "splits" offered as evidence of independence (§3.2) are embodiment artefacts the authors themselves list as weak proxies in §8 — GR00T's T4 = 0 because a rigid box cannot tilt, π0.5's T2 = 1 % because a fixed arm inside a table footprint cannot reach a person at its edge. **Not resting on several tasks:** Table 2 shows T1 measured on exactly one task, and Dynamics on three, one of which (passer-by, 0/25) is a task on which contact cannot occur.

A hostile reader then offers the parsimonious rival: every headline number is a consequence of a single latent fact — *these policies are person-blind*. Present-vs-absent speed is identical (p = 0.79, p ≈ 0.06 in the benign direction); the carry yaw is fixed in the object frame regardless of where the person stands; the arm reaches a person only when the task places the target beside them; the moving-person result is "no reaction to anything". Under this reading the paper measures one absence four times, and the "profile" (§5.5) is a table of scene and embodiment geometry, not of policy behaviour. The main table confirms it: T1 and T5a are 97–100 % for every policy (T5a admitted "scene-set"), so half the columns cannot rank anything, and the remaining differences are driven by n ≤ 4 cells (π0 T1 3/3, T6 3/4; DROID T5b 0/2, T6 0/2) and by the choice of which T3 bearing to headline (100 % at the worst bearing versus 50 % pooled, per Table III's own caption). A benchmark that cannot discriminate between policies is not "leaderboard-ready" (E.8), and the paper says so two sentences earlier: "not a matched head-to-head ranking."

### Ignored Alternative Explanations/Paths
1. **One latent, not four dimensions.** Person-blindness (no conditioning on a person anywhere in the input) predicts every column of Table III; the paper never tests a policy that *does* condition on people, so it cannot show the dimensions dissociate.
2. **Out-of-distribution capability failure.** π0 carries 53/175, DROID 36/110, drawer 0 delivered, handover 2/48: much of what is scored is a policy struggling with the scene. Unsafe motion could be a symptom of distribution shift (Isaac render, capsule, novel objects), not an execution-phase deficit that would survive in-domain fine-tuning.
3. **Action-parameterisation determinism.** T3 and T4 both reduce to "the wrist pose is fixed in the object frame"; that is a property of DROID-style training data, which would make Orientation a dataset finding, not a benchmark dimension.
4. **Scene geometry as the measured variable.** T1 (hazard at the carry midpoint) and T5a (every traversal inside d0) are 100 % by construction; a control policy that is a scripted straight-line carry would score the same, which the paper does not run.

### Missing Stakeholder Perspectives
- The bystander as an agent (people step back, look, speak); the proxies cannot.
- Safety-certification engineers, for whom ISO/TS 15066 and ISO 10218-1 numbers apply after a risk assessment of a specific cell, not as benchmark thresholds.
- Policy trainers / dataset curators, who would need to know whether the defect is fixable with data (nothing here tests it).
- Deployment contexts (home vs industrial), which weight the four columns very differently from equal.

### Unexamined Premise
The paper presumes execution-phase safety should reside in the VLA. Its own evidence points the other way: every witness that fixes a failure (shield, governor, protective stop) is an external layer, and no policy-level intervention (language, rendering) moves any rate. The suite may therefore be measuring a property nobody intends the policy to have.

### Observations (Non-Defects)
- The rotated-spawn manipulation is the strongest single design element; if generalised (rotate every hazardous object, every task) it could become the paper's actual causal spine.
- Reporting threshold curves and contact counts for T2 is the right response to the earlier reviewer and should be the template for T1 and T3 as well.
- The design note's "What this design still lacks" is candid; moving it, verbatim, into §8 would pre-empt half of M1.
