### Summary Assessment
Round 2 concluded that "the *design* is an instrument and the *reporting* is a count." Three days later the reporting has largely been repaired and — more importantly for this round's scope — the **diversity has become load-bearing in places where it was decorative**. Three additions did that work, and they are the best design in the paper: (a) the **scripted straight-line carry control** (§5.5, last row of Table III), which subtracts scene geometry from the policy's share and leaves T4 as the only witnessed, policy-attributable column on the tabletop; (b) the **destination-offset series** (0.32 / 0.45 / 0.55 m from the person's axis: T2 30/160, 2/64, 0/57 right and 8/64, 1/32, 0/25 left, E.8), a monotone dose–response that re-attributes the body-sweep rate from "the policy ignores the person" to "the task puts the destination there" — a conclusion the authors then went back and *qualified in the main text* (commit b6700b6); and (c) the **negative replications** — the fork carried tines-back at ≈177° kills the 180°-spawn effect (11/14 vs 8/9), and the two-bystander cell shows the T3 half-space predicate admits only a knife-edge of compliant directions (1/47), so that cell tests the instrument, not just the policy. A suite whose own diversity narrows its causal claim and audits its own predicate is a designed experiment. Coverage is now large (3 655 tabletop policy episodes) and honestly tiered.

What is still not an instrument is the **headline matrix**, and for reasons that are design-level, not statistical. Verifying Table III against the generator, I find that the "one canonical suite" is a prefix regex over this project's internal run labels, and that it **resolves to a different cell set for each row**: π0.5's T3 pool is 14 cells (scissors and fork, five surfaces, 32/75), GR00T-DROID's is 4 cells (2/10), and the scripted control's is 12 cells of which 8 come from the interaction-geometry battery that is *excluded* for the policies (57/96). On the four cells all rows share, π0.5 is 11/20 = 55 % and the control 16/32 = 50 % — so the **sign of the paper's key attribution comparison reverses** relative to the printed 43 vs 59. T4 and T2 survive matching (84/133 = 63 % vs 16/126 = 13 %; 3/240 vs 2/233), which is exactly why the matched analysis must be the one printed. Separately, the Speed & force column is blank in all five rows (T5a is scored on the G1 only, where n = 6 < the floor of 8), and Trajectory is 50–52 for every fixed-base row by construction, so half the headline matrix carries no information for the tabletop family; roadmap item A3, which would have supplied a scored tabletop speed quantity, was not done. And the coverage apparatus built to answer Round-2 W1 has re-inflated the count in a new currency: Table IV's 42 "tasks" are 10 task types under 32 conditions, §8 says "four are capability boundaries" where Table IV has six (and five more "carried, not delivered"), and Table IVb's caption totals (3 965 / 2 759 / 1 787) are its own rows (3 655 / 2 449 / 1 580) **plus the 310 / 310 / 207 episodes of the scripted control**, for which the table has no column.

None of this needs an experiment. It needs a matched canonical manifest, one arithmetic pass over the coverage table, and a decision about what to do with a dimension that cannot be scored. The paper is close; the claims are one honest re-tabulation away from being the ones the design supports.

## Instrument test

**If a reader wants to rank two policies on execution-phase safety, does this suite let them, and at what confidence?** Partly, at the sub-type level, for two of the four policies, and not at all at the dimension level. Concretely: π0.5 and GR00T N1.6-DROID separate on **T2** (1 % [0, 2] vs 8 % [4, 15]) and on **T6** (94 % [87, 97] vs 56 % [27, 81]); π0.5 and π0 separate on **T4** alone (68 % [62, 74] vs 50 % [37, 63]) and are indistinguishable on T3 (43 % vs 42 %) and T2 (1 % vs 1 %). That is three separating comparisons out of the twelve the suite prints, each resting on a single sub-type. At the **dimension** level the instrument fails outright: the three Franka policies score Orientation 55 / 46 / 51 and Trajectory 50 / 50 / —, while a person-blind scripted IK carrier scores 52 and 36 — the control lands *inside* the policy distribution on three of four columns, which is the paper's own honest reading (§5.5) and is also a statement that those columns cannot order policies. Two structural reasons compound it: the Trajectory score is ≈50 for any fixed-base row by construction (T1 ≈ 100 because the scored marker sits on the direct path; T2 ≈ 0 because a table-side arm cannot reach a standing adult), and Speed & force is blank in all five rows, so **11 of 20 headline cells are formed and 9 are not**. Finally, every tabletop rate is conditioned on *carried*, and the carried fractions differ by a factor of two (π0.5 2 291/3 116 ≈ 73 %; π0 108/359 = 30 %; DROID 50/162 = 31 %), so the rows are computed over different, policy-selected episode populations: part of any apparent safety difference is a competence difference. The honest one-line answer, which the title's word "diagnostic" already licenses: **this suite tells you, with good confidence, *which* execution-phase competence a given policy lacks; it does not yet tell you which of two policies is safer.** Say that in §5, and the empty cells stop being a defect and become a scope statement.

## Strengths

### S1: The control row converts an assertion into a measurement
A scripted differential-IK carry from privileged state, blind to the person (§5.5; 40 cells, 310 episodes, 207 delivered), scored on every predicate, is the cheapest and most decisive design element added since Round 2. It answers DA C1 and my own W3 empirically rather than rhetorically: T1 (100 %), T2 (3 %) and T6 (100 %) are set by scene or task; T4 (13 % against 68 %) is the policy's. Benchmark papers almost never ship a null-competence baseline. This one should be advertised in §4, not discovered in §5.5.

### S2: A dose–response design, which is the rarest thing in a benchmark paper
The bowl-offset series (0.32 / 0.45 / 0.55 m, both sides, plus the counter/desk/packing replications: 30/160, 2/64, 0/57; 8/64, 1/32, 0/25; 0/44, 3/48, 0/48) is a graded manipulation of a single scene parameter with a monotone response, and it **changed a main-text claim** — the "serving raises the body sweep" finding is now correctly attributed to where the task puts the destination. Diversity that falsifies your own earlier sentence is load-bearing by definition.

### S3: Negative replications that narrow a causal claim
The rotated-spawn manipulation was the panel's favourite design element in Round 2; the authors generalised it and it *failed* in three of four new settings (across 3/6 vs 3/8, far-right 2/8 vs 5/10, fork 11/14 vs 8/9, 11/15 vs 6/10), and the claim was correctly restated as "alignment between a frozen carry yaw and the bearing", not "the spawn pose sets the side" (§5.2, E.8). Choosing the fork *because* its carry yaw is near-perpendicular is exactly how a designed factor is supposed to be used.

### S4: The suite audits its own predicate
The two-bystander cell (75 / 47 / 25) shows that under a 90° half-space predicate with people on opposite sides only a knife-edge of directions is compliant — the scripted carrier, deliberately aimed between them, still violates on 8/16 and delivers out of both half-spaces on 2/16 — and E.8 says so in plain words: "this cell is a test of the predicate as much as of the policy." A benchmark that designs a cell to break its own metric has understood what it is building.

### S5: Reactive proxies close the most obvious referee escape route
The withdrawing hand (1 N trigger) refutes "your rates are an artefact of a proxy that cannot move away": the payload follows the retreating hand back to contact on 20/25 episodes, presses ≥ 5 s on 13/95, exceeds 140 N on 4/95, and the effect replicates across three surfaces and two objects and on π0 (10/17 reached, 2/2 followed). This was a B-item and it is fully delivered.

### S6: Tiers, blanks and an explicit unscorable class
Table IV's three tiers, the rule that "a task whose mechanism no predicate captures leaves the cell blank rather than inherit a neighbour's predicate", the eight-episode floor that blocks a dimension mean, and the labelling of midpoint-T1 and tabletop-T5a as *exposure* rather than *unsafe* are all correct benchmark hygiene and all new since Round 2. The willingness to print a blank cell is the single clearest sign that the authors are building an instrument.

### S7: The design note's "what this design still lacks" survived contact with the results
Capability boundaries are now reported as capability boundaries (drill 0/32, pitcher 0/16, push 4/16 carried, door 0/8), and the T5c sensitivity grid (Table IVc: 5–30/55 over threshold × radius) is printed beside the rate rather than in place of it. Nothing here is hidden.

## Major Issues

### W1: The coverage apparatus built to answer Round-2 W1 re-inflates the count, and its arithmetic does not close
**Location**: Table IV (42 rows, E.8 lines ~1046–1090); Table IVb caption; §8 ("31 of 42 tabletop tasks are exercised, four are capability boundaries"); §5.5 ("246 carries"); §4.1's battery list.
**Problem**: Three separate defects in the same apparatus.
(i) **42 "tasks" are 10 task types under 32 conditions.** By verb the rows are pick-and-place (19 rows), serving (10), tool use (4), handover (3), cluttered table, pour, push, drawer, clear-the-table, close-a-door. Rows such as "pick-and-place, other placements", "pick-and-place, person not rendered (perception ablation)", "pick-and-place, surface x map crossed design", "pick-and-place, environment maps", "pick-and-place, rotated spawn at other placements" and "…walker re-timed" are *conditions and ablations of the canonical task*, several of them the very ablations §6 relies on. Counting them as tasks reproduces the Round-2 error in a new currency: the headline number went from "13 tasks" to "42 tasks" while the number of exercised task **types** is five (pick-and-place, serving, cluttered table, pour, and tool use as held-only).
(ii) **§8 mis-states its own table.** Table IV has **six** capability boundaries (drill, pitcher, push, clear the table, close a door, island kitchen) and **five** "carried, not delivered" (two handover variants, the base handover, the drawer, serving at the counter). §8 says "four are capability boundaries" and never mentions the second tier.
(iii) **Table IVb's totals include a non-policy row.** Its caption claims "3965 episodes, 2759 carried, 1787 delivered"; its six surface rows sum to **3 655 / 2 449 / 1 580**. The missing 310 / 310 / 207 is exactly the scripted control's ledger (40 `ik_` cells), i.e. the coverage-per-policy table silently counts a scripted baseline that has no column in it. Its GR00T-DROID row also sums to 165 attempted where §5.5 and E.8 both say 162, and §5.5's "246 carries" for the control disagrees with the ledger's 310.
**Why it matters**: This is the one table a Datasets & Benchmarks reviewer will add up by hand, and it is the table the authors created *specifically* to answer a coverage objection. A caption whose total exceeds its rows by 8 % of the suite, and a limitations paragraph that miscounts its own tiers, will cost more credibility than the coverage gap ever did.
**What would fix it**: Rename Table IV "condition battery" and add a `task type` column with ten values; state "five of ten task types are exercised; 31 of 42 conditions are exercised, six are capability boundaries, five are carried-not-delivered" in §8; recompute Table IVb's caption from its rows and give the control its own column (or exclude it and say so); reconcile 162/165 and 246/310.
**Severity**: Major
**Effort**: 3–4 h, no compute.

### W2: "One canonical suite" is a label regex that resolves to a different cell set in every row of Table III — and the control comparison reverses under matching
**Location**: §4.1 ("Only these cells enter Table III"); §4.2; Table III and IIIb; `tools/gen_a45_numbers.py::canonical()`.
**Problem**: The canonical suite is implemented as a prefix test over internal run labels with hand-written exclusions (`"_cmd"`, `"nocol"`, `"handret"`, `"pitcher"`, `"drill"`, `"_hw_"`, `"_sv_"`, and one hard-coded `l == "t6_hand_s42"`), and the test **branches on the row**: for the scripted control it admits the `ge_` (interaction-geometry) and `kit_t1` prefixes; for the policies it does not. Re-running the generator's own pooling, the T3 pools are
- π0.5: 14 cells — `t3_sci_{L,R}`, `t3_fork_{L,R}`, plus scissors at the drawer, kitchen, office and packing surfaces → **32/75 = 43 %**
- control: 12 cells — `t3_sci_{L,R}` plus **eight** geometry-battery cells (`ge_acr`, `ge_fl`, `ge_fr`, `ge_startR`) → **57/96 = 59 %**
- π0: 5 cells at seeds 1/11/23 → 5/12; DROID: 4 cells → 2/10.

On the four cells every row shares (`t3_sci_{L,R}` × seeds 42/7), **π0.5 is 11/20 = 55 % and the control is 16/32 = 50 %**. The printed comparison (π0.5 43 vs control 59) therefore has the opposite sign to the matched one, and `ge_startR` — the placement where the object *starts beside the person* and π0.5 itself scores 11/12 — is in the control's pool and not in π0.5's. Two further consequences: Table IIIb's canonical denominators do not reconcile with Table IV's canonical row (T3 32/75 vs 39/83; T2 3/365 vs 3/333; T4 159/234 vs 155/228), so a reader cannot tell which pool is the suite; and the per-policy pools differ in *composition*, not just size (π0.5 has the fork and five surfaces, DROID has the dining table alone), so the T3 column compares different mixtures of geometry.
**Why it matters**: §5.5's attribution rule — "where control and policies agree the column is set by scene or task; where they differ the policy owns the rate" — is the paper's central inferential device and the answer to the Round-2 CRITICAL C1. It is valid only on matched cells. As printed, the rule would license the false statement that the control's orientation behaviour is *worse* than π0.5's. It also means no external group can submit a comparable row: the suite is defined by this repository's filenames.
**What would fix it**: (a) Publish the canonical suite as an enumerated manifest (cell id, surface, object, person placement/state, seeds, episode budget, which sub-types it scores) and make `canonical()` read it, so the definition is data and not code. (b) Compute every Table III row on the intersection of cells all rows share, and print the unmatched pools as a labelled secondary; matched cell+seed labels already exist (33 shared with π0, 22 with DROID, 36 with the control), so this costs no compute. The good news is worth stating in the paper: under matching, **T4 (63 % vs 13 %) and T2 (1.3 % vs 0.9 %) hold**, so only T3's printed value moves.
**Severity**: Critical
**Effort**: 6–8 h, no compute.

### W3: One of the four dimensions cannot be scored for any policy, and a second is constant by construction — so half the headline matrix carries no information
**Location**: Table III (all five rows show "—" for Speed & force); §4.2 ("T5a is scored on the mobile G1 only"); §5.3; Round-2 roadmap A3.
**Problem**: The Speed & force score requires {T5a, T5b}; T5a is scored on the G1 only, where n = 6 < the floor of 8; therefore the column is blank for **every policy, including the G1 itself**. A dimension the paper presents as one of four parallel pillars (§3.2, contribution 1, abstract) yields zero scores. Roadmap A3 prescribed the repair — score Annex A.3.3 per-region relative speed on the tabletop from existing logs — and it was not implemented; the tabletop T5a was relabelled *exposure* (correct) but nothing replaced it. Meanwhile Trajectory is 50, 50, 52 for π0.5, π0 and the scripted control, because T1 is ≈100 by construction (the scored marker lies on the direct path: 56/56, 16/16, 16/16) and T2 is ≈0 because a table-side arm cannot reach a standing adult. So for the tabletop family the matrix has one informative column (Dynamics, and only for π0.5) and one partly informative one (Orientation, via T4).
**Why it matters**: The abstract promises rates "pooled by a fixed rule into one score per dimension", and §5 calls Table III the main result. A four-column matrix in which one column is empty for everyone and one is a constant is not the contribution the paper claims; a reader who takes GR00T·G1's Trajectory 89 against π0.5's 50 as a ranking will be comparing a locomoting body sweep with a fixed arm's inability to reach anyone.
**What would fix it**: Either (a) do A3 — give the tabletop a scored speed quantity (relative speed at contact per body region) so the column forms — or (b) say plainly in §3.2 and the abstract that Speed & force is scored on mobile embodiments only and is a two-quantity *exposure* report on fixed-base ones, and drop it from the matrix for those rows. In both cases: mark Trajectory as embodiment-bound, and put a rule in §4.2 that a dimension score is comparable only within an embodiment class. A single horizontal rule in Table III between the G1 and the Franka rows would do most of this work.
**Severity**: Critical
**Effort**: (a) 8–12 h re-scoring, no compute; (b) 2 h of text.

### W4: A canonical cell was re-timed after the data and both versions are pooled into the headline
**Location**: Table IV rows 5 and 6 ("person walks past, office desk and kitchen counter" and "…(walker re-timed)"); E.8; Table IIIb T6b 58/80.
**Problem**: T6b's denominator is an episode subset selected post hoc by a timing test (`mv_in_trans`: the closest approach must fall inside the transport and ≥ 1 s before the place). The criterion is well argued — "a payload slowing to be set down is not yielding" — but at the office desk and the kitchen counter it left 3/20 and 4/17 scorable transports, so the authors **moved the walker 0.60 m nearer** and re-ran, and the headline 58/80 is 28/39 from the original dining-table cell plus 30/41 from the re-timed cells. Two versions of the same scene parameter are pooled into one number, and the original office/counter cells remain in Table IV as a separate row with a blank Dynamics cell.
**Why it matters**: The cell definition changed in response to the data it produced. That is legitimate instrument development and illegitimate pooling: a benchmark must version the scene, report each version separately, and state which version a submitter should run. As printed, 51 % of the T6b denominator comes from a scene revision that is announced only in an E.8 sentence.
**What would fix it**: Version the passer-by cell (v1 launch offset, v2 = v1 − 0.60 m), report T6b per version with n, declare v2 canonical, and state the incidence of the timing window per version (it is itself a scene-design statistic worth reporting: 39/68, then 21/28 and 20/22).
**Severity**: Major
**Effort**: 2–3 h, no compute.

### W5: For a Datasets & Benchmarks submission the protocol section is not yet a deliverable
**Location**: §7 (four sentences); Appendix H; Reproducibility Statement; Appendix C.
**Problem**: §7 promises scenes, recorders, logs and "a policy is a server swap", and Appendix H puts the canonical thresholds to the community as *open questions* ("whether T3 should be elevated…", "we propose publishing versioned defaults"). What is missing is everything a second lab needs: the enumerated canonical cell manifest (W2), a suite version number, a licence, a datasheet, the per-cell episode budget and seed list actually used (Appendix C says "eight episodes per cell and seeds 42 / 7", which is false for the cells carrying the headline — the ledger shows seeds 1, 3, 7, 11, 23, 31, 42 and cells of 2 to 16 episodes), a scoring-script contract with an input schema, and a statement of what a submitter must report for a row to be admissible. A benchmark whose defaults are still open questions cannot accumulate results, which is the only reason to build one.
**Why it matters**: This is the venue-fit issue. On the D&B track the artifact and its protocol *are* the contribution; the four findings are the evidence that the instrument detects something. Right now the findings are strong and the artifact is under-specified, which inverts the track's weighting.
**What would fix it**: Promote the manifest of W2 into §7 as "Suite v1.0"; state the versioned defaults instead of proposing them; add a one-page datasheet (scenes, objects, proxies, licence, known limitations, what is *not* covered); state the admissibility rule for a submitted row (all canonical cells, ≥ 8 episodes per cell, the seeds in the manifest, Wilson intervals, tier declared).
**Severity**: Major
**Effort**: 8–12 h, no compute.

### W6: The diversity that is decorative is now labelled, but the environment maps still occupy headline real estate they have not earned
**Location**: §4.1 ("four environment maps"), Table IV rows 24 and 26, Table IVd, E.8 "A crossed surface × map design (next-cycle probe)".
**Problem**: The maps have moved from the headline to a battery row and a one-seed crossed design (Table IVd: 2 surfaces × 3 maps × 2 objects, 8 episodes per cell), and the authors correctly call the tilt difference (mug > 45° on 8/8 under the lounge map, 3/8 under the courtyard) "a live hypothesis, not a result". That is honest. But the maps still appear in §4.1's battery list, in two Table IV rows (57 and 89 episodes) whose T3/T4 numbers are printed as rates, and in the figure gallery — and no predicate in the paper is designed to respond to illumination. Round-2 SC-14 (CONSENSUS-4) asked for them to be a one-cell robustness check; they are now a two-cell robustness check whose one interesting signal is confounded with a single seed. Appendix A7's instruction to state that the outdoor maps are tonemapped previews rather than HDRs was not carried into the paper at all (the string does not appear).
**Why it matters**: Not much on its own — but a D&B reviewer counting axes will see "six work surfaces × four maps" and expect a crossed design; the paper should pre-empt that read, and it should not print T3 88 % (15/17) for a "environment maps" row as if the map were the factor.
**What would fix it**: One sentence in §4.1 — "environment maps are a visual-domain robustness check on two surfaces at one seed; no predicate is referenced to illumination, and the outdoor maps are tonemapped previews, not HDRs" — and convert the two Table IV map rows to counts with the seed stated.
**Severity**: Minor–Major (borderline; listed here because it is the axis the panel voted 4–0 on in Round 2)
**Effort**: 1 h.

### W7: Cross-policy rates are computed over policy-selected episode populations, though matched cells exist
**Location**: §4.2 (denominators), Table IIIb, Appendix F "Coverage", §5.5.
**Problem**: Every tabletop rate conditions on *carried*, and the carried fractions are 73 % (π0.5), 30 % (π0) and 31 % (DROID). So π0's T4 50 % and DROID's T6 56 % are measured on the third of episodes those policies managed to lift at all — plausibly the easier geometries. Appendix F states the coverage asymmetry and says the cross-policy claim is "the recurrence of a profile, not a ranking", which is the right disclaimer; but Table III still prints three comparable-looking numbers per column, and §8 does not name selection as a threat. The fix is available for free: the ledger contains 33 cell+seed labels shared by π0.5 and π0 and 22 shared with DROID.
**Why it matters**: This is the difference between "the profile recurs" (supportable) and "these are three policies' scores" (what the table looks like). It also matters for the one place the paper does compare policies on a claim — T2 1 % vs 8 % — where DROID's higher rate could be a property of the 31 % of episodes it carried.
**What would fix it**: A matched sub-table over the shared cells, with the unmatched pools relegated; one sentence in §8 naming completion-dependent selection as a threat to the cross-policy comparison.
**Severity**: Major
**Effort**: 4–6 h, no compute.

## Final Recommendation

**Minor Revision**, conditional on items 1–3 above. Confidence 4.

The panel's Round-2 finding was that the design was an instrument and the reporting a count. The reporting has been substantially repaired and the diversity has been converted from a count into a designed experiment: eleven of sixteen axes now carry a conclusion, three of them carry a conclusion that *contradicts an earlier draft of the paper*, and the scripted control gives the suite something almost no benchmark ships — a measurement of how much of its own signal is scene geometry. That is the work of an instrument-builder, and it should be said so in the decision letter.

What remains is bounded and clerical in the best sense: the canonical suite must become data rather than code so that its rows can be matched (without which the control row must be withdrawn, and my recommendation would revert to Major); one of the four dimensions must either acquire a score or lose its column; and the coverage table must add up. If those three are done, I would sign an accept on the design and diversity of this suite, with the caveat — which the title's word "diagnostic" already carries and which §5 should state outright — that it diagnoses one policy at a time and does not yet rank two.
