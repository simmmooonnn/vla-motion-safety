\section{Benchmark}
\label{sec:bench}

\subsection{Scenes, tasks and policies}
\label{sec:scenes}

The tabletop scenes run in Isaac Sim~6.0 with Isaac Lab~3.0 \citep{ref14} and IsaacLab-Arena~0.3 \citep{ref15}.
The robot is a Franka Panda in the DROID configuration \citep{ref65}: an external and a wrist camera, and absolute joint-position actions at 15\,Hz.
It works at five surfaces (Fig.~\ref{fig:scenes}): a dining table, a kitchen counter, a drawer kitchen (the same counter with an open drawer), an office desk and a packing station; a sixth, an island kitchen, contributes one cell.
\pifive{} and \pizero{} run at all five, \pifast{} at four and \grd{} at three.
A still bystander, usually an adult 1.74\,m tall, stands at the surface (Table~\ref{tabB:surfaces}).
In every scored cell this person is rendered as a capsule body and has no collider; a human mesh was checked for T3 only (App.~\ref{app:d-t3}).
At the kitchen counter and the drawer kitchen the bystander stands outside both policy cameras' frames, so those cells score the carry, not a reaction to a visible person (Fig.~\ref{figB:view}).
As a robustness check, three further light maps (auto shop, courtyard, lounge) at the kitchen counter and packing station give \pifive{} 48/60 on T3 (default maps, same placement: 8/10) and 1/96 on T4 (0/32).
They change lighting and backdrop, not the room.

Pick-and-place into a bowl is the goal of 93\,\% of scored cells; pouring, a drawer task and stirring add 41 cells, on \pifive{} and, for the drawer, \pifast{}.
A hot-plate marker or the bystander's resting forearm beside the transport line is T1's keep-out.
A bowl beside the person creates T2's serving geometry.
Scissors or a fork carried past the person give T3 a hazardous axis, and a mug gives T4 a load to keep level.
For Dynamics, a forearm capsule with a collider and a contact sensor crosses the transport line at 0.40\,m/s (T6a), and a walker capsule up to 1.22\,m tall passes at 0.55\,m/s (T6b).

As a case study, \gone{} \citep{ref5,ref13} drives a Unitree G1 humanoid on a shelf-to-bin box carry, about 1.9\,m down a corridor, with the same predicates around it.
Its T1 keep-out is a hot stove beside the path; a live electrical strip on the path is exposure.
It has no person-blind control and no witness for T2--T4, so its row is reported, not compared (App.~\ref{app:g1}).

\textbf{Policies.}
We evaluate four public DROID-trained checkpoints, run unmodified behind Arena's policy runner.
\pifive{} \citep{ref63} is openpi's base DROID joint-position checkpoint \citep{ref67}, without simulated co-training.
\pizero{} \citep{ref62} and \pifast{} \citep{ref64} are PolaRiS checkpoints, co-trained on simulated data \citep{ref66}.
\grfull{} is NVIDIA's DROID fine-tune of GR00T N1.6-3B \citep{ref13}.
The three openpi policies execute action chunks of 0.67 or 1.0\,s open loop (App.~\ref{appB:policies}).
The rows differ in backbone, action decoder and training data at once, so we compare each with the person-blind control and credit no difference between rows to one factor.
Episodes end when the environment's success condition holds or after 35\,s; \grd{} runs 90\,s on almost all of its T1--T4 cells because its carries often begin later.
The tabletop benchmark logs 11,849 attempted episodes; its task library has 55 tasks, of which \pifive{} can do 42 (Table~\ref{tabB:library}).
The supplementary video shows rendered episodes for T1, T2, T3, T6a and T6b and the humanoid case study.

\subsection{Protocol and statistics}
\label{sec:protocol}
\label{sec:stats}

Box~1 defines the terms and units.
Episodes in a cell are correlated, so every 95\,\% interval is a Wilson interval on a sample size discounted for that clustering.
A policy is tested against the control only on placements both ran: we report the matched difference with a cluster-robust interval and permute whole cells.
Holm correction is applied within each of five test families (App.~\ref{app:stats}).
Three analyses were pre-registered: the T2 serving tests, the joint-space control and a replication on fresh seeds.
Their protocols are time-stamped files in the release, frozen before their cells ran, not a public registry; all other contrasts are exploratory.
Per-cell results appear only in the release, whose table of 667 cells regenerates the policy, control and witness rates of Table~\ref{tab:main}.
