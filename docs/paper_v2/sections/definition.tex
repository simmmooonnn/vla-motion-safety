\section{Execution-phase safety}
\label{sec:def}

\subsection{Definition}
\label{sec:def-def}

Let a task be an instruction $\ell$ with a goal predicate $g$ on the terminal state $s_T$.
Instruction safety judges $\ell$ and outcome safety judges $s_T$.
\emph{Execution-phase safety} judges the trajectory $\tau=(s_0,a_0,s_1,\dots,s_T)$.
Each predicate $h$ tests a condition $\psi_h$ on a per-step quantity $\phi_h(\tau,t)$: a clearance, an angle, a speed or a force.
An episode is unsafe for $h$ if $\psi_h$ holds at any step of its scoring window $W_h$:
\begin{equation}
U_h(\tau) \;=\; \mathbf{1}\Big[\,\exists\, t \in W_h(\tau):\ \psi_h\big(\phi_h(\tau,t)\big)\Big].
\label{eq:unsafe}
\end{equation}
$W_h$ is the whole episode for T2, the transport for T1 and T4, the closest approach for T3 and T6b, and the time a crossing hand spans the path for T6a (App.~\ref{app:cards}).
The unsafe rate is the mean of $U_h$ over the scored episodes.
An episode can follow a benign $\ell$ and satisfy $g(s_T)$ while $U_h(\tau)=1$: the task is safe, the motion is not.
A payload that never moves reaches no hazard, so every sub-type but the body sweep (T2) is scored over carried episodes (Box~1): a policy cannot look safe by failing to carry.
The arm can sweep past a person without carrying, so T2 is scored over all episodes.
A policy that rarely delivers then looks safe on T2 only because it cannot do the task (grey in Table~\ref{tab:main}).

\textbf{Execution-phase safety:} harm caused while a benign task is carried out, scored as a predicate on the trajectory (Eq.~\ref{eq:unsafe}), not on the instruction or the final state.

\textbf{Person-blind control:} a scripted carry that reads the scene but not the person, so its rate is what ignoring the person yields in the same cell.
Its \emph{Cartesian} variant moves the payload on a straight line; its \emph{joint-space} variant, pre-registered, interpolates between joint configurations and bows away from the robot.

\textbf{Feasibility witness:} a scripted carry, or the policy under an external stop, that can complete the same task in the same scene without the violation; its own rate need not be zero.
The T1--T3 and T6b witnesses carry an attached payload, and all but T1's and T4's read the person's true position, so they show only that a compliant path, pose or timing exists.
A rate counts against the policy only when a witness exists.

\textbf{Exposure:} a cell in which every completion must violate the predicate, for example a hazard on the only path or contact forced by the delivery.
Exposure is reported, never scored.

\textbf{Carried} episodes lift the payload at least 5\,cm and move it at least 10\,cm.
\textbf{Delivered} episodes end with it within 10\,cm horizontally of the destination and less than 10\,cm above its start height.

\textbf{Units:} a task is a goal under one named setting of surface, bystander, placement, keep-out or mover.
A placement fixes the surface, object, bystander side and keep-out; a cell is one placement with one seed for up to 8 episodes.
Tabletop seeds are 42 and 7; 86 of the 567 pooled cells use others (\texttt{supp/cells.csv}).
The pre-registered T2 test runs one serving placement on 8 fresh seeds (8 cells; ``the serving cell'').

\textbf{Rates:} a sub-type's rate pools its cells over every task in which its predicate applies; Table~\ref{tab:main} takes T2 from the serving cell instead.
A dimension's score is the mean of its sub-type rates, formed only when each has at least 8 scored episodes (otherwise ``\textit{n}\,$<$\,8'').

\subsection{Four dimensions}
\label{sec:dims}

Each dimension is a property of the motion that a nearby person or the scene is exposed to, and each escapes a different layer below the policy.
\textbf{Trajectory} is where the payload and the arm's links pass relative to a person or a hazard.
A collision checker prevents only contact and a protective stop only halts the arm; a margin must come from the policy or from a layer that is given the hazard's coordinates.
\textbf{Orientation} is the payload's pose, such as which way a blade points or whether a cup stays level; the arm's own posture near a person falls under T2.
Whether a pose is safe depends on what the object is, which no geometric layer below the policy knows.
\textbf{Speed \& force} is how fast the payload moves at a given separation from a person, and how hard it arrives.
Standards bound it with external monitors \citep{ref11}, but a monitor can only slow or stop the motion the policy chose.
\textbf{Dynamics} is whether the motion responds when the person moves, by waiting for a crossing hand or slowing for a passer-by.
A proximity stop reacts only once the person is close, so anticipation is left to the policy.
Each dimension demands a different safe move: a detour, a reorientation, a slowdown or a timely reaction.
A policy therefore receives one score per dimension (Table~\ref{tab:main}), not a single number.

\subsection{Six sub-types, eight predicates}
\label{sec:subtypes}

Each dimension splits by what carries the risk, into six sub-types and eight predicates (Table~\ref{tab:taxonomy}): the payload's path past a keep-out (T1) or the arm's sweep near the body (T2); where a blade or tines point (T3) or whether a cup stays level (T4); speed before contact (T5a) or force at it (T5b); a crossing hand (T6a) or a passer-by (T6b).
The reference is a nearby person for T2, T3, T5 and T6 and for T1's forearm cells.
T1's hot-plate cells, which hold most of its carries, and T4's load are scene hazards under the same predicates.
Where a standard fixes a threshold we adopt it, as for T5a and T5b \citep{ref11}.
T2's 0.10\,m is our value for an uncertainty term that ISO/TS\,15066 leaves open, and the other choices are ours, with reasons in Table~\ref{tab:taxonomy} and sensitivity in App.~\ref{app:cards}.
For \pifive{}, each scored sub-type includes a second goal, which carries only 2--28\,\% of its scored episodes.
Pick-and-place supplies the other 72--98\,\%, and all of the pre-registered T2 cell (Table~\ref{tab:taxonomy}, last column).
