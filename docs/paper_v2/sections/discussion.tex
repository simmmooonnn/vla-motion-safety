\section{Discussion}\label{sec:disc}

\textbf{The competence is probably missing from the training data.}
VLA policies imitate demonstrations selected for task completion.
A teleoperator carrying a mug past an inert prop has no reason to detour, and no one scores which way the scissors point.
If the competence is absent from the data, a safety instruction has nothing to retrieve and can trigger a related skill: the spill clause tilts the mug, plausibly toward a pour.
Two concurrent results fit this reading: fine-tuning on human-present demonstrations elicits yielding \citep{ref73}, and a navigation VLA encodes pedestrians it does not act on \citep{ref79}.
Our ablations fit it too but do not establish it.

\textbf{The policy sets the demand on the safety layer.}
A certified system does not rely on the policy for its safety function, but the policy decides how often that function must act.
With a whole-arm protective stop, \pifive{} never reaches the crossing hand and still delivers the mug, but the stop fires on every carry and holds the arm for a median of 2.9\,s.
Some failures have no external fix at all: a stop does not change which way a blade points or how far a cup tilts.

\textbf{What a new policy should report.}
A low unsafe rate alone is not evidence of safety.
A policy claiming execution-phase safety should report each sub-type on matched cells against a person-blind control, with its carry rate, and show by a witness that the predicate can be met.
Half of our matched comparisons give evidence in neither direction, so larger matched cells come first.

\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limits}

{\setlength{\leftmargini}{1.1em}%
\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}\setlength{\parskip}{0pt}\setlength{\topsep}{0pt}
\item \textbf{Simulation and proxies.} All results are simulated, and every person proxy is static or kinematic. With no injury model, contact and force counts are exposure, not harm.
\item \textbf{A capsule, not always in view.} In scored cells the person is a capsule, and at two of the five surfaces no policy camera shows it (18\% of \pifive{}'s T3 carries). A human mesh was checked for T3, on small samples, and for the humanoid's crossing person (12/12 reached), not for the serving cell, the crossing hand or the wording experiments, so person-blindness may partly be capsule-blindness.
\item \textbf{Small cells.} A tabletop cell holds up to eight episodes, so only large contrasts have power.
\item \textbf{Narrow tasks and scenes.} Pick-and-place is the goal of 93\% of scored cells and the only one for \pizero{} and \grd{}. Dynamics is not scored at the packing station or the drawer kitchen.
\item \textbf{One training corpus.} All four policies are trained on DROID, and rows differ in backbone, decoder and data at once, so no row difference is attributed to one of them.
\item \textbf{Reference-dependent T1, thin T2.} The T1 excess over the Cartesian control largely disappears against the joint-space control, which only \grd{} exceeds. T2 rests on one pre-registered placement with a thin margin, read against the Cartesian control only.
\item \textbf{Unequal protocols.} \grd{} ran 90\,s episodes on most of its T1--T4 cells, and the humanoid case study has one corridor and no person-blind control.
\end{itemize}}
\noindent The scorers do register compliant behaviour: the witnesses read 0--10\% (25\% for T6b), and a whole-arm stop takes \pifive{}'s T6a from 15/15 to 0/16 on the dining-table mug cells. What we do not show is a learned policy that achieves this from camera input; every witness reads privileged state.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:concl}

Most VLA safety evaluations ask whether a task should be done and whether it ended well; we measure how it is carried out, in four dimensions, most of them referenced to a nearby person.
Read against person-blind controls and feasibility witnesses, no public DROID-trained policy we test is detectably safer than a carry that ignores the person, and safety language can make the carry worse.
What a policy owns is not the safety function but the demand it places on it, which can now be counted dimension by dimension.
Next steps are a positive control, such as a policy fine-tuned on human-aware demonstrations, and reactive human proxies with an injury model.
